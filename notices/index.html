Black Friday and £100 energy payment scam warning

West Mercia Police's November fraud bulletin warns that fake Black Friday deals are being offered all month. Buy only from retailers you trust, check website addresses carefully, avoid links in unexpected emails and be wary of pressure to act quickly or pay by bank transfer.

More

Criminals may also use the one-off £100 energy payment due to eligible households in January 2027: only your own council will contact you about it, so never give bank details to anyone who calls, texts or emails. If you have been scammed, contact Report Fraud on 0300 123 2040.

Harvington parish council news: Leys Road verge, Jubilee Orchard path and a new flower bed

Please don't park on the grass verge on Leys Road: it churns up the grass, stops contractors cutting it and must be kept clear for the defibrillator there.

More

A sign on the Jubilee Orchard gate now says the farmland next to it is private. The path beside it is not a public right of way and the overhanging trees are outside the orchard, so the parish council's powers are limited; the landowner appears happy for dog walkers to use the path. Worcestershire County Council's website shows which paths are public rights of way.

The new flower bed on the green beside the village hall was arranged by the parish council and will be planted and looked after by Limebridge Rural Services, who also tend the village planters. Some older planters that are rotting are being replaced.

Reverend Caroline's letter from St Egwin's — November

Last month the harvest service and lunch were both well attended. We had been unsure as to whether the Harvest lunch would be able to be held, but the builder did all he could to get our hall open as soon as possible.

More

So, although we thought numbers might be down everyone was keen to come along and villagers, congregation and visitors from other local churches sat down and enjoyed casseroles and apple pies. The previous day the hall celebrated reopening with cakes and fizz and it was lovely to see a diverse group of people attending and getting to know each other to support the hall. It was well attended. The hall is now fully open and groups can return when they are able. The coffee mornings we hold in village hall and church are an essential part of community life, offering a place where all can meet and share time which is vital in the support of community for each one of us.

On October 21st St Egwins hosted Bishop Hugh's Deanery gathering. He wanted to meet people from our Deanery, this is all of the churches in towns and villages around and including Pershore and Evesham. At first, we thought about 35 to 50 people would attend. Our church was chosen because we have a car park, toilet and facility to make tea. It was a very busy evening as over 100 people came to join us and meet Bishop Hugh. He is a very animated and inspiring man and everyone enjoyed the opportunity to meet him and he them. He has other gatherings around the Diocese to meet people from each area, share Worship and hear them. I want to thank all who brought cakes; amazingly we did not run out and I was very grateful for the generous contributions.

In November we begin working towards Christmas, and our craft group will be looking at what we have for sale at the Christmas fair. It is a social meeting when we meet and have coffee and a chat while planning items to make and sell at the fair.

The St Egwins Christmas fair begins our December season on 5th December at the village hall starting at 2pm. As we move towards our first full year in benefice together, we look forward to working more collaboratively together: everything from arranging to cut costs on regular checks for things like PAT testing, if they can be arranged for all of the churches on the same day, to ensuring dates don't clash so that we can support one another at fund raising events.

Reverend Caroline Green, Associate Priest, St Egwin's Church, Norton and Lenchwick

Neighbourhood Watch says burglaries tend to rise as the evenings draw in, and asks residents to look out for vulnerable neighbours. With more Christmas parcels arriving, have deliveries left somewhere hidden, with a neighbour or at a collection point rather than on the doorstep.
